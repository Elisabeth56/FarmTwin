# Chunk: Maize growth stages overview

Maize progresses through five recognizable stages from planting to harvest. Germination typically occurs 4 to 7 days after planting, provided soil moisture and temperature are adequate. The seedling stage follows and lasts roughly 2 to 3 weeks, during which the plant establishes its root system and produces its first true leaves. The vegetative stage is the longest, typically running from about week 3 to week 8 or 9 after planting, and is when the plant builds most of its biomass and leaf area. Flowering (tasseling and silking) then occurs, usually between weeks 8 and 10 in early-to-medium maturing varieties. The grain-filling and maturity stage follows, ending in physiological maturity roughly 90 to 120 days after planting depending on variety. Total time from planting to harvest for common Nigerian maize varieties is typically 90 to 120 days for early varieties and 120 to 140 days for medium-duration varieties.

# Chunk: The vegetative stage in detail

The vegetative stage of maize begins once the seedling has established (roughly three weeks after planting) and continues until the plant begins to tassel, typically around eight to nine weeks after planting for medium-duration varieties. During this stage, the plant produces new leaves at a steady rate, extends its stem, and expands its root system. Nutrient uptake, particularly nitrogen, is at its highest during the middle and late vegetative stage. The plant is visibly growing week to week; by the end of the vegetative stage, a healthy maize plant in favorable conditions can reach 1.5 to 2.5 meters in height. This stage is also when the plant is most responsive to management interventions such as nitrogen top-dressing and weeding, and it is when several important disease and pest pressures reach their peak.

# Chunk: Transition from vegetative to reproductive stages

The transition from vegetative to reproductive growth is a critical window in maize. Tasseling — the emergence of the male flower at the top of the plant — is followed within a few days by silking, the emergence of the female silks from the developing ears. This transition, typically occurring 55 to 65 days after planting in medium-duration varieties, is when the plant becomes most vulnerable to water stress and heat stress. Damage during this window has an outsized effect on final yield because it directly reduces the number of kernels that will form. Farmers whose crops are approaching this stage should prioritize any actions that reduce plant stress: ensuring adequate soil moisture where possible, and managing any active pest or disease pressure decisively before flowering begins.

# Chunk: Maize planting calendar in Northern Nigeria

In Northern Nigeria, including Kaduna State, the main maize growing season aligns with the rainy season, which typically runs from May through October. Planting is usually done between late May and mid-June, once the rains have established consistently. Planting too early risks germination failure if a dry spell follows; planting too late risks the crop reaching maturity after rains stop, which can affect grain filling. For a June 12 planting date typical of the middle-belt maize regions, the vegetative stage runs approximately from early July through mid-August, flowering occurs in late August, and harvest typically falls in September or October depending on variety maturity duration. This calendar aligns most naturally with medium-duration varieties suited to the region's rainfall pattern.
