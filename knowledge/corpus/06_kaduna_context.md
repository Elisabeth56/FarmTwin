# Chunk: Kaduna State agroecological profile

Kaduna State lies in the Northern Guinea Savannah agroecological zone of Nigeria. The climate is characterized by a distinct wet season (typically May through October) and a dry season (November through April), with total annual rainfall generally in the range of 900 to 1400 mm, most of it falling during the wet season. Temperatures during the growing season typically range from 22 to 32 °C. The zone supports rain-fed cultivation of a range of staple crops including maize, sorghum, millet, cowpea, and groundnut, with maize particularly important in the southern parts of the state where rainfall is higher. Soils are predominantly sandy loams to loams of moderate fertility, with organic matter content often depleted in areas of long continuous cultivation. Kaduna hosts Ahmadu Bello University, a major agricultural research institution for the region.

# Chunk: Rainfall patterns and variability in Kaduna

The rainy season in Kaduna typically begins with the first substantial rains in April or May and builds to its peak in July and August, then tapers through September and October. August is typically the wettest month, with monthly rainfall commonly exceeding 250 mm. The season is characterized by significant year-to-year variability in both timing and total amount. Early-season dry spells after initial rains can affect germination and stand establishment. Late-season shortfalls can affect grain filling in longer-duration varieties. Farmers manage this variability primarily through variety selection (matching maturity duration to expected season length) and planting timing (waiting for consistent rainfall establishment before planting). Recent decades have seen increased variability that makes traditional planting calendars less reliable in some years.

# Chunk: SAMMAZ maize varieties overview

The SAMMAZ series (Samaru Maize) is a family of maize varieties released by the Institute for Agricultural Research (IAR) at Ahmadu Bello University in collaboration with IITA, targeting Nigerian and West African growing conditions. Varieties in the series span early, medium, and late maturity durations and incorporate breeding for regional disease pressures (including Northern Corn Leaf Blight and rust), Striga tolerance, and drought tolerance in some lines. SAMMAZ varieties are widely adopted in Northern Nigeria and are typically the recommended baseline for maize production in Kaduna and surrounding states. Early varieties in the series (roughly 90-100 days to maturity) suit shorter rainy seasons and higher-latitude areas; medium varieties (110-120 days) are common in the middle-belt including much of Kaduna; late varieties are used where rainfall is most reliable.

# Chunk: Choosing a variety for a specific farm

Variety choice for a specific farm balances several factors: expected length of the growing season based on location, soil moisture holding capacity, farmer preference for grain type (white or yellow), market demand, and disease resistance profile relevant to observed local pressures. In Kaduna, medium-duration SAMMAZ varieties are the common baseline. Where rainfall onset is uncertain or where a farmer plants late, an early-maturing variety may be a safer choice to ensure the crop reaches maturity before rains end. On fields with a history of specific disease pressure (Northern Corn Leaf Blight, for example), selecting a variety with strong resistance to that disease is one of the highest-leverage decisions available to the farmer at planting time.
