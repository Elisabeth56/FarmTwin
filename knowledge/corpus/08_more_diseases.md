# Chunk: Downy mildew symptoms and conditions

Downy mildew of maize, caused primarily by Peronosclerospora species, develops under warm humid conditions with prolonged periods of leaf wetness or high overnight humidity. Symptoms include yellowish streaks or chlorotic bands running along the length of leaves, often with a white fuzzy growth (the pathogen's spore-producing structures) visible on the underside of affected leaves during periods of high humidity. Infected plants may be stunted, and severely infected plants can fail to produce viable ears. The disease is systemic in some forms — meaning once the plant is infected, the pathogen is present throughout — so early-season infection is more damaging than late-season infection. Seed treatment with appropriate fungicides is a common preventive measure where the disease is a recurring problem, and resistant varieties are the most sustainable control.

# Chunk: Ear rots and grain quality risks

Ear rots caused by Fusarium, Aspergillus, and other fungi develop during grain filling and maturation and can substantially affect grain quality even when yield in terms of ear number and size appears acceptable. Symptoms include discolored kernels (pink, white, or greenish molds visible when husks are pulled back), poor kernel development, and in severe cases visible fungal growth on the ear. Weather conditions favoring ear rot include prolonged warm humid conditions during and after silking, and delayed harvest that leaves mature ears exposed to further weather. Some ear rots produce mycotoxins (aflatoxin from Aspergillus, fumonisin from Fusarium) that are harmful to human and animal health, making affected grain unsuitable for consumption. Management includes timely harvest, proper drying to below 14 percent grain moisture, and clean storage. Ear rots are more relevant later in the season than during the vegetative stage.

# Chunk: Comparing foliar disease symptoms

Quick visual differentiation of common foliar diseases on maize: Northern Corn Leaf Blight produces long elliptical gray-green to tan lesions 3-15 cm long running parallel to leaf veins, starting on lower leaves. Common rust produces small (2-5 mm) cinnamon-brown pustules that release spores when rubbed. Gray Leaf Spot produces narrow rectangular gray-brown lesions running between veins, distinctive because the parallel-vein-boundary shape is unlike the other diseases. Downy mildew produces long yellowish or chlorotic streaks running along leaves, sometimes with white fuzzy growth on undersides. Feeding damage from fall armyworm is often mistaken for disease at first glance — the ragged edges and holes with visible frass distinguish it. When in doubt, sending a photograph or physical sample to an extension officer or research station is worthwhile for accurate diagnosis.
