# Chunk: Rice growing basics in Nigeria

Rice is a major staple in Nigeria with production spread across a range of ecologies including rainfed lowland, upland, and irrigated systems. The crop cycle from planting to harvest typically runs 100 to 150 days depending on variety. Rainfed rice production dominates in areas without irrigation infrastructure and is highly weather-dependent. Key production challenges include water management (particularly aligning the crop's peak water demand with reliable rainfall or ensuring adequate irrigation), weed pressure, and blast disease under humid conditions. Nigerian research institutions including the National Cereals Research Institute have released rice varieties adapted to local conditions across the range of ecologies.

# Chunk: Cassava growing basics

Cassava is a long-duration root crop typically grown for 9 to 15 months from planting to harvest, tolerant of poor soils and drought conditions where many other crops would fail. It is propagated from stem cuttings rather than seed. The main pests and diseases of concern include cassava mosaic disease (caused by viruses spread by whiteflies) and cassava brown streak disease in some areas. IITA has released improved cassava varieties widely used across West Africa that combine higher yields, disease resistance, and better processing qualities. Cassava is central to food security in many Nigerian communities and is used both as a fresh vegetable and processed into gari, fufu, and other products.

# Chunk: Yam growing basics

Yam is a culturally and economically important tuber crop in Nigeria, particularly in the middle belt often called the "yam belt." The crop is propagated from seed yams (small tubers) planted at the start of the wet season, typically March or April. The growing cycle spans most of the rainy season with harvest usually from November through January. Yam requires deep well-drained soils and benefits from staking to allow the vines to climb. Major challenges include the cost and availability of seed yam, anthracnose disease, and post-harvest tuber deterioration. Improved varieties from IITA and Nigerian research institutions have targeted yield improvements and disease resistance, though adoption of improved varieties has been slower than for some other crops.
