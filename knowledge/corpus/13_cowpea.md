# Chunk: Cowpea planting, maturity and weeding

Cowpea varieties fall into maturity groups by days from planting to maturity: extra-early (about 60 to 69 days), early (70 to 79), medium (80 to 89) and late (90 to 120). Sowing is timed to the onset of the rains, and the guide gives late July to August as the planting window. Weeds compete hardest early in the season, so the guide recommends weeding at about 2 weeks and again at 4 to 5 weeks after planting. Knowing the variety's maturity group tells a farmer when flowering and podding will fall, which is when most pest damage happens.

# Chunk: Cowpea insect pests and spray timing

Insects are the main yield limit for cowpea. Aphids attack seedlings, and the guide places the first spray against them at about 14 to 21 days after planting. Flower thrips and the pod borer Maruca strike at flowering, so the key spray is at flower bud initiation, around 30 to 35 days after planting, followed by a second spray about 10 days later. Where Maruca or pod-sucking bugs are heavy, a third spray follows another 10 days on. Pod-sucking bugs alone can cause losses of 30 to 70 percent. Scouting the field at budding and flowering is how a farmer decides whether those later sprays are needed.

# Chunk: Striga and storage in cowpea

The parasitic weed Striga gesnerioides attaches to cowpea roots and is worse where rainfall is low and soils are poor, so it is a bigger problem in the drier savanna. After harvest, the grain should be dried to below 10 percent moisture before storage. Bruchids (storage weevils) are the main storage pest, and well-dried grain in sealed storage holds up much better against them.
